## What actually moved

If you buy carbide inserts, carbide punches, carbide gauge pins or carbide wear plates, the line that fell on your quote sheet this year has not been quiet. China's tungsten carbide powder price fell for most of the year after a spring spike, and then it turned around in the first two weeks of November.

On 13 November 2026, tungsten carbide powder was quoted at $117.52-121.99 per kg in China, according to pricing tracked by Ruixin Tungsten Carbide from Shanghai Metals Market data. Eleven days earlier, on 2 November, the same reference price was $111.57-116.04/kg. That is the quietest kind of number — a dealer price sheet, not a headline — but it is a real, dated, five to six percent move in under two weeks.

| Date (2026) | China tungsten carbide powder, USD/kg |
|---|---|
| 2 Nov | $111.57-116.04 |
| 3 Nov | $114.55-119.01 |
| 6 Nov | $116.04-120.50 |
| 9 Nov | $117.52-121.99 |
| 13 Nov | $117.52-121.99 (unchanged) |

The same tracker ties the move to a specific trigger: a Guangdong producer raising contract prices, as reported by SMM on 10 November 2026. It is not a broad commodity panic — one major producer moved its contract number, and the reference price followed within days.

It is not an isolated data point either. In the same window, Asian Metal's index showed ferro-tungsten at 70% and 75% minimum grades up 2.07% and 2.01% respectively, as of 3 November 2026, and metal.com's tracking showed tungsten bar priced at $124.43-132.37/kg, up $1.41, as of 6 November 2026. Different products in the same supply chain, moving the same direction, in the same fortnight.

## The bigger arc this sits inside

This November uptick follows a much sharper move earlier in the year, worth remembering before anyone overreacts to a two-week chart. China APT has fallen from a March peak of about RMB 1.52 million a tonne to roughly RMB 595,000, and tungsten powder from about RMB 2,400 to RMB 920 a kg by the autumn correction. Put plainly: a record spike in March, a real correction by September, and now a fresh move upward off that corrected base.

Whether the November move is the start of another climb or a one-off contract adjustment is not something a tool room in Chennai or Bengaluru can call from a price tracker. What a tool room can do is notice that the direction changed, and plan accordingly.

## Why this reaches your BOM even if you never buy carbide directly

Most tool rooms in South India don't import tungsten carbide powder. They buy finished carbide blanks, ground inserts, rod stock and brazed tips from local and regional distributors, who price off these same international benchmarks with a lag of a few weeks to a couple of months. A move that shows up in a China trade price in early November tends to reach a distributor's quote around the time many buyers are placing orders for the new quarter.

Carbide shows up in tooling in specific, usually small, places: punch tips and die buttons in blanking and piercing stations, wear inserts in [progressive dies](/capabilities/press-tools/), locating and wear pins in [inspection gauges](/capabilities/gauges-and-spm/), and cavity inserts in [mould tooling](/capabilities/plastic-moulds/) running abrasive, glass-filled resins. None of these use kilos of the stuff. But carbide is usually the single most expensive material line on a tooling bill of materials per kilogram, so even a 5-6% move changes the arithmetic on a quote more than the same percentage move on the tool steel body ever would.

## What to actually do about it

A few things a buyer can control, regardless of which way the carbide price goes next:

- **Separate the material line on every quote.** If a supplier gives you one number for a progressive die, ask them to break out the carbide content separately. You can't manage what you can't see.
- **Ask what grade and how much is really needed.** A full carbide insert at a high-wear shear edge is often justified; a carbide insert on a low-cycle, low-volume job sometimes isn't. That's a design conversation, not a cost-cutting one — it belongs at the quotation stage, before steel is cut.
- **Time bulk buys around your own production calendar, not the market.** Guessing a commodity bottom is a distraction for a tool room. Buying the carbide needed for tools already on order, on a predictable schedule, is not.
- **Get the quote itemised.** An itemised quotation lets you see the carbide line move independently of labour, design and tool steel, so the next price revision doesn't arrive as a surprise buried in a lump sum.

## What to do next

If a die, gauge or mould tool with carbide wear components is coming up for quotation, ask for that line itemised before you commit. It costs nothing to ask, and it's the only way to see a move like this coming instead of finding it in next quarter's invoice.
